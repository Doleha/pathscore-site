CMA (IMA) vs. CPA: Comparing the Cost and Path for Accounting Credentials (2026)

By The PathScore Team Updated November 23, 2026 6 min read

Both credentials open doors in accounting and finance, but they are built differently: CMA is a two-part exam run through one organization with one published fee schedule, while CPA licensure runs through state boards, so the per-section fee is consistent but the total cost depends on which state you sit in.

Quick verdict

Want a two-part exam from one organization, with one published fee schedule? Take the CMA from IMA: a $300 entrance fee, then $545 per part for Part 1 and Part 2, listed as the non-member price on IMA's enrollment page. Membership is also required, at $295 per year. Want the license needed to sign audit reports and practice public accounting? Take the CPA: $268.59 per exam section, paid to NASBA, with total state fees varying by board.

The two are not mutually exclusive. CMA focuses on management accounting and financial strategy inside a company, while CPA licensure is required for public accounting and audit work, and some readers hold both.

Deep dive

CMA (IMA)

The CMA is run by IMA (Institute of Management Accountants) and has two parts: Part 1 (Financial Planning, Performance, and Analytics) and Part 2 (Strategic Financial Management). IMA's enrollment page lists a $300 entrance fee and $545 for each part, and labels those prices as non-member prices. The same page says the CMA Program is available to active IMA members and that you need an active membership before you buy the entrance fee or an exam.

IMA's Professional membership costs $295 per year and lists CMA enrollment among its features. At the listed prices, a first year of membership, the entrance fee and both parts comes to $1,685 before study materials. The enrollment page does not show a separate member price for the exams, so we did not confirm whether members pay less per part.

CPA

CPA licensure is administered through state boards of accountancy, and the exam fee itself is paid to NASBA. Using Virginia as a reference point, the Virginia Board of Accountancy's fee page lists the exam fee at $268.59 per section, as of July 4, 2026, and a $120 non-refundable application fee. The CPA exam has multiple required sections, so the exam cost is the per-section fee multiplied by the number of sections a candidate sits, plus whatever application and registration fees that candidate's own state board sets.

The NASBA per-section fee is the same nationally, but the total cost of becoming a CPA varies by state because each board sets its own additional fees.

Pros & cons

CMA (IMA): Pros

  • One governing body and one published fee schedule on IMA's enrollment page: $300 entrance, $545 per part
  • Only two exam parts to budget for and schedule
  • No state-by-state fee structure to navigate

CMA (IMA): Cons

  • Membership is required, so the first-year cost at listed prices is $1,685 before study materials
  • The listed exam prices are labeled non-member prices, and the member exam price is not shown on the pages we read
  • Does not grant a license to sign audit reports or practice public accounting the way CPA does

CPA: Pros

  • Required license for public accounting, audit sign-off, and many senior finance roles
  • Per-section NASBA fee is a known, nationally consistent figure ($268.59, as of July 4, 2026)
  • Broadly recognized credential across every state

CPA: Cons

  • Total cost varies by state, since each board sets its own application and other fees on top of the NASBA exam fee
  • Multiple required exam sections mean the per-section fee adds up before a candidate finishes all of them
  • Licensure, not just passing the exam, typically also requires state-specific education and experience requirements, which this page does not cover

Who each one is for

CMA (IMA) fits best for: readers focused on management accounting, financial planning, and corporate finance roles, who want a two-part exam with one published fee schedule rather than a state-by-state process, and who are willing to pay for IMA membership as part of the cost.

CPA fits best for: readers who need the license to sign audit reports, practice public accounting, or meet a state requirement for certain senior finance and accounting roles, and who are comfortable budgeting for a per-section fee that is the same nationally even though total state costs vary.

FAQ

Is the CMA cheaper than the CPA?

It depends on how many CPA sections you need and which state's fees apply. At the listed prices, the CMA costs $1,685 in the first year, including the required membership, the $300 entrance fee, and two parts at $545 each. The CPA costs $268.59 per section through NASBA, plus your state board's application and other fees, so the CPA total depends on the state and on how many sections you take.

Do I need IMA membership to take the CMA?

Yes, according to IMA's enrollment page. It says the CMA Program is available to active IMA members and that you need an active membership before you buy the entrance fee or an exam. Professional membership costs $295 per year. The pages we read do not show a separate member price for the exams, so we did not confirm whether members pay less per part.

Why does the CPA exam fee vary by state?

The $268.59 per-section fee is paid to NASBA and is the same nationally, as stated on the Virginia Board of Accountancy's fee page (as of July 4, 2026). Each state board sets its own application, registration, and other fees on top of that, so the total out-of-pocket cost differs by state.

Can I hold both CMA and CPA?

Yes. They serve different purposes: CMA focuses on management accounting and financial strategy inside an organization, while CPA licensure is required for public accounting and audit work. Some readers pursue both over time.
