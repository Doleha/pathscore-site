Two vendor-specific firewall certification tracks that are genuinely current and comparable, now that Palo Alto's PCNSA/PCNSE have both been retired.

Important note on Palo Alto Networks: If you've seen older content recommending Palo Alto's PCNSA or PCNSE, that advice is now outdated. We confirmed directly against Palo Alto's own certification page that neither exam can be newly registered anymore; PCNSE retired July 31, 2025, and PCNSA retired even earlier. Palo Alto now issues a different, role-based tier structure (Cybersecurity Apprentice/Practitioner, Network Security Professional, and similar). Existing PCNSA/PCNSE holders keep their credential for 2 years from issue, but neither is a viable new purchase today. This comparison covers the two vendor-certification tracks that are genuinely current and comparable: Check Point and Fortinet.

Deep dive

Check Point CCSA and CCSE

Check Point's training path costs meaningfully more than Fortinet's at a comparable tier: a 3-day CCSA course runs around $2,100 versus Fortinet's far cheaper self-study-friendly path to NSE 4. We confirmed both Check Point exam fees directly from the vendor: CCSA's $300 figure comes from Check Point's own official exam prep guide, and CCSE's fee, previously only an inference based on CCSA's pricing pattern, is now also confirmed at $300 directly from Check Point's own CCSE Exam Prep Guide (noting it may vary by region or testing center).

Fortinet NSE 4–6 and NSE 7+/Industry

Fortinet's pricing is sourced directly from the vendor's own help desk pages and is the most reliable figure in either track: NSE 4 through NSE 6 rises from $200 to $210, and NSE 7 and the Industry certifications rise from $400 to $420, both effective November 2, 2026. For the full tiering breakdown, see our separate Fortinet NSE tiers guide.

These certifications validate product-specific skills more than general security knowledge; a CCSA credential doesn't transfer meaningfully to a FortiGate-based network, and vice versa. Salary data for both vendors is thin and mostly aggregator-sourced; treat the rough bands as directional only, since none come from a named, independent primary source.

FAQ

Is Check Point CCSE's $300 exam fee confirmed?

Yes. We checked Check Point's own CCSE Exam Prep Guide directly: the fee is $300, matching CCSA's own documented price. Check Point's own wording notes it may vary by region or testing center, so a quick re-check at booking is still worth doing.

Can I still get Palo Alto's PCNSA or PCNSE?

No. We confirmed directly against Palo Alto's own certification page that neither exam can be newly registered anymore; PCNSE retired July 31, 2025, and PCNSA retired even earlier. Existing holders keep their credential for 2 years from issue, but it isn't a viable new purchase today.
