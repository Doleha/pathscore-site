Deep dive

Terraform Associate (004)

Terraform Associate tests the current Terraform 1.12 syllabus in a one-hour, multiple-choice, online-proctored format for $70.50 plus local taxes, with no free retake. It is the most affordable credential in this comparison and the one most directly tied to a single tool rather than a whole orchestration platform, which matters if your team is Terraform-first but cloud-agnostic. One change worth flagging before you commit to this path: HashiCorp has opened registration for a new Terraform Infrastructure Engineer Professional exam, positioned a step above Associate. Readers aiming for the top Terraform credential available should check HashiCorp's own certification page for the Professional exam's requirements rather than assuming Associate is the ceiling.

CKA, CKAD, CKS

The Linux Foundation's Kubernetes certifications are priced at $445 per exam when purchased individually. Unlike the multiple-choice formats above, these are performance-based: you work in a live terminal against real clusters, which is a large part of why employers treat them as a stronger signal of hands-on ability. CKA (Certified Kubernetes Administrator) is the baseline and the one most job postings name directly. CKAD (Application Developer) targets people building and deploying applications on Kubernetes rather than administering the cluster itself. CKS (Security Specialist) is the advanced tier and has a hard prerequisite: you must hold a valid CKA before you can sit CKS. Buying more than one at once is meaningfully cheaper than buying them separately: CKA+CKAD or CKA+CKS bundles run $825, and all three together run $1,245, a $90 discount versus the $1,335 you would pay buying each at full price.

KCNA

KCNA (Kubernetes and Cloud Native Associate) is the Linux Foundation's entry-level, no-prerequisite credential, priced at $250, well under CKA's $445. It is multiple choice rather than performance-based, which makes it a lower-stakes way to validate foundational Kubernetes and cloud-native knowledge before investing in CKA's hands-on exam.

Pros & cons

Terraform Associate (004): Pros

  • Lowest price in this comparison at $70.50 plus tax
  • Short, one-hour format, cloud-agnostic across AWS, Azure, and GCP

Terraform Associate (004): Cons

  • No free retake
  • Now a mid-tier credential rather than HashiCorp's top Terraform exam, since the new Professional exam has opened for registration

CKA / CKAD / CKS: Pros

  • CKA is the credential most Kubernetes job postings name specifically
  • Performance-based format is a stronger signal of real cluster experience; bundle pricing saves $90 versus buying all three separately

CKA / CKAD / CKS: Cons

  • $445 per exam is the highest per-credential cost here
  • CKS cannot be taken until you hold a valid CKA

KCNA: Pros

  • No prerequisite, and the cheapest Kubernetes-track credential at $250
  • Multiple choice, lower-pressure format than CKA's live terminal exam

KCNA: Cons

  • Multiple choice format carries less weight with employers than CKA's performance-based exam
  • Does not substitute for CKA if a job posting specifically requires it

Who each one is for

Terraform Associate fits best for: engineers and consultants who work across multiple cloud providers and need a cloud-agnostic, low-cost credential that proves infrastructure-as-code competence quickly.

CKA (plus CKAD and/or CKS) fits best for: engineers who already run or will soon run production Kubernetes clusters and need the specific credential employers screen for, with CKS as a follow-on once cluster security work is part of the job.

KCNA fits best for: people newer to Kubernetes and cloud-native concepts who want a lower-cost, lower-risk credential to validate the basics before deciding whether to invest in CKA.

FAQ

Is Terraform Associate still HashiCorp's top Terraform credential?

No. HashiCorp has opened registration for a new Terraform Infrastructure Engineer Professional exam, a step above Associate. Associate remains the standard entry point, but it is no longer the ceiling.

Do I need CKA before I can take CKS?

Yes. CKS has a hard prerequisite: you must hold a valid CKA.

Is KCNA a substitute for CKA on a resume?

Not for roles that specifically require CKA. KCNA is a no-prerequisite, multiple-choice credential meant as an entry point, while CKA is the performance-based credential most Kubernetes job postings name directly.

Are there bundle discounts for the Kubernetes exams?

Yes. The Linux Foundation offers CKA+CKAD or CKA+CKS at $825 and CKA+CKAD+CKS at $1,245, a $90 savings versus the $1,335 combined price of buying all three individually.
