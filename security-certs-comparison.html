Deep dive

CompTIA Security+

The default first security cert: no prerequisites, and the only one of the three with a hard compliance mandate (DoD 8570/8140) behind it. Payscale reports a $90K avg base (U.S., 6,144 responses); the honest role-level baseline is BLS Information Security Analysts median $129,180 (May 2025 OOH).

Certified Ethical Hacker (CEH)

Built for hands-on offensive security / pentesting-track professionals. Payscale reports $96,490 avg base, with a wide early-career spread ($78,614 entry-level vs. $100,416 early-career). Budget for the jump in cost if you don't yet have 2 years of experience, since you'll be forced into EC-Council's official, more expensive training path.

(ISC)² CISSP

For experienced security practitioners moving into management/architecture roles. (ISC)²'s own salary page reports a median of $150,000 (North America) and $127,000 (global), based on its Cybersecurity Workforce Study, the strongest reported median pay of the three, but genuinely gated behind 5 years of real domain experience. Don't let anyone sell you a "CISSP in 3 months with no experience" path.

Pros & cons

CompTIA Security+: Pros

  • No prerequisites, open to total newcomers

CompTIA Security+: Cons

Certified Ethical Hacker (CEH): Cons

  • Forces expensive official training if you lack 2 years of experience

(ISC)² CISSP: Pros

(ISC)² CISSP: Cons

  • Ongoing $135/yr maintenance fee

Who each one is for

FAQ

Which security cert should I get with zero experience?

CompTIA Security+: it's the only one of the three with no prerequisites, and it's also DoD 8570/8140-mandated for many federal and contractor roles, which neither CEH nor CISSP is at the entry level.

Can I get CISSP without 5 years of experience?

No legitimate path skips this. CISSP requires 5 years of paid experience across at least 2 of its 8 domains (or 4 years plus a qualifying degree). Unlike Security+ and CEH, which has an alternate paid-training path for people without the 2-year experience requirement, CISSP's experience requirement has no substitute.
