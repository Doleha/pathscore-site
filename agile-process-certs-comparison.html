Deep dive

Certified ScrumMaster (CSM)

Requires paying a licensed Scrum Alliance trainer for a mandatory 2-day course: there's no self-study path and no way to sit an "exam only." Payscale reports $115,465 avg base (U.S., 2026), close enough to PSM I and ITIL Foundation's figures that salary isn't a useful differentiator between the three. Recurring 2-year renewal (~$100 + 20 SEUs) adds ongoing cost.

Professional Scrum Master I (PSM I)

The cheapest way to make existing Scrum knowledge official: $200, once, ever, with the free official Scrum Guide as the primary prep material. No mandatory course, no renewal fee. Payscale reports $110,000 avg base (1,189 responses), within the same ballpark as CSM and ITIL Foundation.

ITIL Foundation

Sits in between CSM and PSM I on cost: not usually mandatory-course-gated like CSM, but pricier than PSM I, with pricing that varies meaningfully by ITIL version (v4 vs. v5) and region. Payscale reports $114,000 avg base (7,804 responses). It's a different discipline from CSM/PSM: relevant for IT service management (ITSM) rather than software Scrum teams specifically, despite appearing in the same "agile/process" bucket.

Pros & cons

Certified ScrumMaster (CSM): Pros

  • Most commonly named Scrum credential in job postings
  • Live instructor-led course includes built-in exam attempt

Certified ScrumMaster (CSM): Cons

  • No self-study path: mandatory paid trainer course, $995–$1,500+
  • Recurring 2-year renewal fee (~$100 + 20 SEUs)

Professional Scrum Master I (PSM I): Pros

  • $200 one-time cost, no renewal fee, ever
  • Free official study guide (the Scrum Guide) covers the material

Professional Scrum Master I (PSM I): Cons

  • No live instructor support if you're new to Scrum concepts
  • Less commonly named in job postings than CSM despite similar pay

ITIL Foundation: Pros

  • Not usually mandatory-course-gated like CSM
  • Directly relevant to ITSM career tracks, not just software teams

ITIL Foundation: Cons

  • Pricing varies meaningfully by version (v4 vs. v5) and region; confirm before purchasing
  • Not really a CSM/PSM competitor if you're not in ITSM

Who each one is for

Certified ScrumMaster (CSM) fits best for: candidates whose employer is paying and specifically requires CSM, or who are targeting Scrum-Alliance-oriented shops.

Professional Scrum Master I (PSM I) fits best for: candidates paying their own way who already know Scrum in practice and want the lowest-cost path to a credible credential.

ITIL Foundation fits best for: professionals in IT service management (ITSM) roles rather than software Scrum teams specifically.

FAQ

Which of these three pays the most?

None of them, meaningfully: the three Payscale averages ($110K–$115K) are close enough that salary is not a useful differentiator between CSM, PSM I, and ITIL Foundation. The data suggests these certs track with the role you're already in more than they cause a pay jump on their own.

Is ITIL Foundation a competitor to CSM or PSM I?

Not really, despite appearing in the same "agile/process" bucket. ITIL Foundation is relevant for IT service management (ITSM) roles, while CSM and PSM I are Scrum-specific. If you're working in ITSM rather than software Scrum teams, ITIL Foundation is the right cert, not a CSM/PSM substitute.
