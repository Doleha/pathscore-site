Quick verdict

Price-check flag, confirmed: CPA's per-section exam fee rose to $268.59 (from $262.64) effective July 4, 2026, confirmed via multiple state board of accountancy pages citing NASBA's own fee schedule. Committing to the full multi-year CFA program? The charterholder compensation data is a reasonable signal of the eventual payoff, but budget on years, not months. Want to work directly with individual clients, with the strongest salary data of any credential here? CFP has the clearest documented pay premium. Want the broadest, most universally recognized accounting credential? CPA remains the default.

Deep dive

CFA Level 1

Every salary number available for CFA describes a full charterholder, someone who has passed all three levels, not someone who has passed Level 1 alone. CFA Institute runs a real, large-scale Compensation Study, but its detailed figures sit behind a member-only login, so the numbers above are secondary-sourced and carry a "reportedly" qualifier rather than confirmed primary data. If you're specifically evaluating whether Level 1 alone is worth pursuing, none of these figures answer that directly; they describe the outcome of finishing the entire program, which typically takes several years. Separately, Robert Half's 2026 Salary Guide describes CFA as "valued but not required" for financial analyst roles in the $65,000–$92,000 range, without quantifying a specific premium, a more honest picture of what Level 1 alone likely signals to employers. CFA's exam fees, by contrast, are the most reliably sourced of any cert in this comparison, coming directly from CFA Institute's own published schedule.

CFP (Certified Financial Planner)

CFP Board publishes its own compensation data directly, unusually strong sourcing compared to most certs PathScore has covered. One contamination warning worth repeating: two secondary sources found during this research actually describe an unrelated healthcare credential (NAHQ's CPHQ) under CFP-branded URLs and titles, with a roughly $85,000 salary figure that has nothing to do with the real CFP designation. Don't use any CFP salary figure attributed to a non-cfp.net source without double-checking it.

CPA (US, Certified Public Accountant)

We confirmed the CPA per-section fee increase directly: multiple state boards of accountancy (Virginia and Nebraska, among others) cite NASBA's $268.59 fee effective July 4, 2026. AICPA also publishes its own compensation data directly, and its total-compensation figure is meaningfully higher than the more commonly cited base-salary number from PayScale.

Pros & cons

CFA Level 1: Pros

  • Most reliably sourced exam fees of any cert in this comparison, straight from CFA Institute
  • Signals serious commitment to a rigorous, globally respected program

CFA Level 1: Cons

  • No salary data exists specifically for Level 1 alone; all figures describe full charterholders
  • Full program takes years, not months, to complete

CFP: Pros

  • Best-sourced, highest salary figure in this entire comparison, direct from CFP Board
  • Clear tenure-based pay progression data

CFP: Cons

  • Standard path prep cost can run as high as $11,759

CPA: Pros

  • Broadest, most universally recognized accounting credential
  • AICPA's own total-compensation figure ($161,535) beats the commonly cited base-salary number

CPA: Cons

  • Per-section fee just rose to $268.59 as of July 4, 2026, confirmed via NASBA

Who each one is for

CFA Level 1 fits best for: candidates committing to the full multi-year charter program who understand that Level 1 alone signals commitment to employers rather than directly changing pay.

CFP fits best for: anyone who wants to work directly with individual clients on financial planning and wants the strongest documented salary premium of the three.

CPA fits best for: candidates who want the broadest, most universally recognized accounting credential, with AICPA's own total-compensation figure as the number to cite.

FAQ

Is the CPA fee increase to $268.59 confirmed?

Yes. We confirmed it via multiple state boards of accountancy citing NASBA's own fee schedule: the per-section fee rises from $262.64 to $268.59 effective July 4, 2026.

Does passing CFA Level 1 alone boost my salary?

There's no dedicated salary data for Level 1 passers specifically. Every available figure describes full charterholders who passed all three levels, a multi-year commitment. Treat Level 1 as a signal of commitment to employers rather than an immediate pay lever.
